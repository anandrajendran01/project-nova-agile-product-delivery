# Project Nova

## Agile Digital Customer Experience Enhancement

**Delivery Model:** Scrum / Agile  
**Duration:** 6 Months  
**Core Cross-Functional Team:** 10 Members  
**Sprint Cadence:** 2 Weeks  
**Planned Sprints:** 12  
**Product Area:** Digital Customer Self-Service  
**Investment Model:** Fixed team / timebox with prioritized variable scope  
**Status:** Portfolio Case Study

## Project Overview

Project Nova is an independent project-management and Agile-delivery case study demonstrating the end-to-end delivery of a digital customer experience enhancement using Scrum.

The initiative enhances an existing customer self-service platform by improving order visibility, returns and refund journeys, proactive notifications, customer preferences, and service transparency.

The case study is designed to demonstrate how a Project Manager / Scrum Master / Agile Delivery Lead can manage product outcomes, sprint execution, dependencies, risks, release confidence, stakeholders, financial guardrails, and production transition without converting Scrum into a predictive project model.

## Business Context

The existing digital customer-service experience has functional gaps that drive avoidable contact-center demand, inconsistent status visibility, manual follow-up, and customer frustration.

Project Nova aims to improve customer self-service while reducing operational effort and increasing digital completion of priority service journeys.

## Product Outcomes

The initiative will target measurable improvement in:

- Digital completion of priority customer-service journeys.
- Order and return status transparency.
- Customer notification effectiveness.
- Reduction in avoidable service-contact volume.
- Release predictability and delivery flow.
- Production quality and customer experience.

## Delivery Approach

Project Nova uses Scrum with a two-week sprint cadence and a prioritized Product Backlog.

The delivery model is based on:

- A stable cross-functional team.
- Product Owner-led backlog prioritization.
- Sprint Goals rather than task-level commitment.
- Incremental product delivery.
- Empirical planning using capacity, velocity, quality, and outcome data.
- Continuous refinement and reprioritization.
- Explicit dependency, risk, and release governance.
- Controlled production rollout and post-release stabilization.

Detailed scope is expected to evolve as the team learns. Material changes to investment, delivery window, regulatory obligations, or major external commitments require governance; ordinary backlog reprioritization does not require predictive-style change control.

## Project Management Areas Demonstrated

**Product Vision & Outcomes**  
Business problem, customer outcomes, value hypotheses, success measures, and product goals.

**Backlog Management**  
Epics, features, user stories, acceptance criteria, prioritization, refinement, Definition of Ready, and Definition of Done.

**Sprint & Release Planning**  
Capacity planning, sprint goals, release forecasting, dependencies, and incremental delivery.

**Agile Metrics & Forecasting**  
Velocity, throughput, carry-over, sprint-goal achievement, burn-up, defect trends, release confidence, and forecast ranges.

**Stakeholder & Product Governance**  
Product Owner accountability, stakeholder engagement, decision rights, demos, reviews, escalation, and product decisions.

**Risk & Dependency Management**  
Cross-team dependencies, technical risks, operational constraints, release risks, and action ownership.

**Financial Guardrails**  
Team-based cost model, investment tracking, forecast-to-complete, contingency, and cost-per-sprint / release visibility.

**Quality & Release Management**  
Definition of Done, test automation, defect governance, non-functional acceptance, release readiness, production rollout, and hypercare.

**Continuous Improvement**  
Retrospectives, root-cause analysis, flow improvements, working agreements, and measurable delivery improvement.
