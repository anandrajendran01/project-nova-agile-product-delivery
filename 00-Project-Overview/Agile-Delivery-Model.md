# Project Nova — Agile Delivery Model

**Framework:** Scrum  
**Cadence:** Two-Week Sprints  
**Planned Delivery Horizon:** 12 Sprints / 6 Months  
**Core Team:** 10 Members  
**Primary Control Model:** Empirical delivery + product outcomes + investment guardrails

---

## 1. Delivery Philosophy

Project Nova uses Scrum because the initiative contains meaningful uncertainty around customer needs, feature priority, UX choices, technical implementation, and the relative value of individual backlog items.

The delivery model is designed to:

- Deliver usable increments frequently.
- Obtain feedback early.
- Reorder work based on evidence.
- Protect team focus during the Sprint.
- Forecast using actual delivery data.
- Make scope trade-offs rather than automatically extending time or investment.

---

## 2. Scrum Accountabilities

### Product Owner

The Product Owner is accountable for maximizing product value and managing Product Backlog ordering.

Key responsibilities include:

- Product Goal ownership.
- Backlog ordering.
- Acceptance alignment.
- Business-value decisions.
- Stakeholder alignment.
- Scope trade-offs within product guardrails.

### Scrum Master / Agile Delivery Lead

The Scrum Master / Agile Delivery Lead supports effective Scrum adoption and delivery flow.

Key responsibilities include:

- Facilitation.
- Impediment removal.
- Delivery transparency.
- Dependency escalation.
- Metrics interpretation.
- Continuous improvement.
- Coaching Product Owner and team.
- Release / stakeholder coordination where required.

### Developers / Cross-Functional Team

The cross-functional team is accountable for creating a Done Increment.

The team determines how selected Sprint Backlog work is delivered and adapts the plan during the Sprint as new delivery information emerges.

---

## 3. Scrum Events

### Sprint

Two weeks.

The Sprint provides the primary delivery and inspection cadence.

### Sprint Planning

Sprint Planning establishes:

- Why the Sprint is valuable.
- The Sprint Goal.
- Which Product Backlog Items are selected.
- How the team plans to create the Increment.

Selection is constrained by actual capacity and evidence—not by pressure to fill every available story point.

### Daily Scrum

Used to inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt the team's delivery plan.

The Daily Scrum is not treated as a management status meeting.

### Sprint Review

The team and stakeholders inspect the Increment and discuss what changed in the product / business environment.

The Product Backlog may be adapted based on feedback.

### Sprint Retrospective

The team inspects its way of working and identifies a small number of actionable improvements.

Improvement actions are tracked rather than recorded only as discussion notes.

---

## 4. Backlog Hierarchy

Project Nova will use a simple hierarchy:

**Product Goal → Epic → Feature / Capability → User Story / Enabler**

The case study will avoid unnecessary hierarchy where it does not improve decision-making.

Backlog ordering will consider:

- Customer value.
- Business value.
- Risk reduction.
- Dependency sequence.
- Technical enablement.
- Effort / complexity.
- Learning value.
- Release need.

A formal prioritization model will be introduced later.

---

## 5. Sprint Commitment Model

The team commits to the **Sprint Goal**, not to an inflexible guarantee that every selected story must finish regardless of changing information.

Incomplete work is not automatically carried over.

At Sprint end, unfinished items return to the Product Backlog for re-evaluation by the Product Owner.

This prevents recurring carry-over from becoming normalized.

---

## 6. Definition of Ready

A lightweight Definition of Ready will be used as a refinement aid, not as a rigid Scrum gate.

A backlog item should normally have:

- Clear user / business intent.
- Testable acceptance criteria.
- Known major dependencies.
- Sufficient design / technical context.
- Size appropriate for a Sprint.
- No unresolved blocker that makes Sprint selection irresponsible.

The Product Owner and team may still use judgment where uncertainty is intentional and learning is part of the work.

---

## 7. Definition of Done

A Product Backlog Item is Done only when the agreed quality criteria are satisfied.

The Nova Definition of Done is expected to include:

- Acceptance criteria satisfied.
- Peer review complete.
- Automated / manual testing complete as applicable.
- No unresolved critical defect.
- Security / privacy checks complete where applicable.
- Analytics / telemetry included where required.
- Documentation updated.
- Integrated into the working product.
- Product Owner / acceptance evidence available where appropriate.

Release approval may require additional enterprise controls beyond Definition of Done.

---

## 8. Delivery Metrics

Nova will use metrics to support decisions, not to rank individuals.

Core delivery measures will include:

- Sprint Goal success rate.
- Velocity trend.
- Capacity versus planned commitment.
- Carry-over rate.
- Backlog burn-up.
- Throughput.
- Cycle time where useful.
- Escaped defects.
- Defect aging.
- Dependency aging.
- Release forecast confidence.

Metrics will be interpreted as a system, not in isolation.

---

## 9. Product Metrics

Delivery metrics answer:

**“How effectively are we delivering?”**

Product metrics answer:

**“Is the delivered product creating the intended outcome?”**

Nova will therefore pair delivery metrics with customer / business measures such as self-service completion, service-contact reduction, customer journey completion, and experience metrics.

Exact targets will be established separately.

---

## 10. Forecasting

Nova will not assume constant velocity before evidence exists.

Early forecasts will use:

- Team capacity assumptions.
- Initial relative estimates.
- Dependency information.
- Historical / comparable delivery evidence if available.

After several Sprints, forecasts will increasingly use actual Nova performance.

The forecast should express confidence and assumptions rather than a false single-date certainty.

---

## 11. Release Governance

Nova distinguishes between:

- Done backlog items.
- Potentially releasable Increment.
- Release candidate.
- Production authorization.
- Production deployment.

Release decisions consider product value, dependency readiness, defect position, production support, security / privacy, and operational controls.

A material release decision remains a governance decision even in an Agile delivery model.

---

## 12. Continuous Improvement

Retrospectives will produce small, owned improvement actions.

Examples may include:

- Better refinement.
- Smaller stories.
- Test automation.
- Earlier dependency engagement.
- Reduced WIP.
- Improved environment stability.
- Clearer acceptance criteria.
- Faster defect feedback.

Improvement will be measured through delivery behavior rather than the number of retrospective actions created.

---

## 13. Relationship to Project Atlas

Project Atlas demonstrated predictive control around:

- Scope baseline.
- Integrated schedule.
- BAC.
- EVM.
- Formal change control.
- Stage-gate deployment.

Project Nova deliberately demonstrates a different management model:

- Stable delivery team.
- Timeboxed Sprints.
- Product Backlog adaptation.
- Empirical forecasting.
- Product / customer outcomes.
- Scope trade-offs.
- Agile release governance.
- Continuous improvement.

Both approaches maintain governance and financial discipline, but they control different variables.
