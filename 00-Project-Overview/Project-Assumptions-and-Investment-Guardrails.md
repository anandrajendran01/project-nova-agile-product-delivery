# Project Nova — Project Assumptions & Investment Guardrails

**Portfolio Case Study:** Agile Digital Customer Experience Enhancement  
**Delivery Model:** Scrum / Agile  
**Duration:** 6 Months  
**Sprint Length:** 2 Weeks  
**Planned Sprints:** 12  
**Core Cross-Functional Team:** 10 Members  
**Approved Investment Envelope:** ₹1.25 Crore  
**Scope Model:** Prioritized / Variable Product Backlog  
**Status:** Synthetic Portfolio Case Study

> All project data, financial values, delivery metrics, backlog items, risks, and outcomes in Project Nova are synthetic and created solely for portfolio demonstration.

---

## 1. Purpose

Project Nova demonstrates how a product-oriented Agile initiative can be governed without treating every backlog change as a formal project change request.

The project operates with:

- A stable six-month delivery horizon.
- A stable cross-functional team.
- A two-week sprint cadence.
- A controlled investment envelope.
- A prioritized Product Backlog that can evolve based on customer, business, technical, and operational learning.

The project therefore manages **investment and outcomes more tightly than detailed scope**.

---

## 2. Core Assumptions

### Product Context

Nova enhances an existing digital customer self-service experience.

The product area includes capabilities such as:

- Order visibility.
- Returns and refund transparency.
- Proactive notifications.
- Customer preferences.
- Self-service support.
- Service-status transparency.

The initiative enhances an existing platform rather than building a completely new product from zero.

### Delivery Horizon

The project runs for approximately six months using twelve two-week sprints.

The delivery cadence is expected to include:

- Ongoing Product Backlog refinement.
- Sprint Planning.
- Daily Scrum.
- Sprint Review.
- Sprint Retrospective.
- Continuous integration and testing.
- Incremental production releases where readiness allows.

### Team Stability

A ten-person cross-functional Scrum team is assumed to remain broadly stable for the delivery period.

Detailed role allocation, RACI-style decision boundaries, and working agreements will be established separately.

### Existing Enterprise Dependencies

Nova depends on existing enterprise capabilities such as:

- Identity and customer profile services.
- Order-management services.
- Returns / refund services.
- Notification infrastructure.
- Analytics / telemetry.
- Production support and release-management functions.

These dependencies are not assumed to be under the Scrum team's complete control.

## 3. Investment Guardrail

The approved synthetic investment envelope for the six-month initiative is:

**₹1.25 Crore**

This is an **investment guardrail**, not a predictive BAC in the Project Atlas sense.

The envelope is intended to cover the planned Nova delivery horizon, including:

- Core cross-functional team cost.
- Product / UX effort.
- Engineering and quality effort.
- Platform / cloud consumption attributable to the initiative.
- Limited specialist support.
- Release and stabilization effort.
- Controlled delivery contingency.

A detailed sprint-level cost and capacity model will be established later in the case study.

## 4. What Is Fixed vs Adaptive

### Relatively Fixed

The following are treated as management guardrails:

- Six-month delivery horizon.
- Two-week sprint cadence.
- Ten-person core team.
- ₹1.25 Crore investment envelope.
- Product vision and business outcomes.
- Mandatory security, privacy, accessibility, and production-control obligations.
- Major enterprise release constraints.

### Adaptive

The following are expected to evolve:

- Detailed backlog scope.
- Story sequencing.
- Epic decomposition.
- Acceptance-detail refinement.
- UX design choices.
- Technical implementation options.
- Release content within approved product boundaries.
- Lower-value backlog items.

This distinction is central to Project Nova.

---

## 5. Backlog Change vs Formal Escalation

Normal backlog reprioritization does **not** require formal project change control when it remains within the agreed product boundaries, team capacity, delivery horizon, and investment guardrail.

Examples of normal Agile adaptation include:

- Moving a story to a later sprint.
- Splitting a story.
- Reordering backlog items.
- Removing lower-value functionality.
- Adding a newly discovered story that replaces lower-priority work.
- Refining acceptance criteria.
- Adjusting implementation approach.

Formal management escalation is required when a proposed change is expected to materially affect one or more of the following:

- Approved ₹1.25 Crore investment envelope.
- Six-month delivery horizon.
- Major external / contractual commitment.
- Mandatory regulatory, privacy, security, or accessibility obligation.
- Critical enterprise architecture constraint.
- Major product objective.
- Material production / operational risk.

---

## 6. Financial Governance Assumption

Project Nova does not use Earned Value Management as the primary delivery-control method.

Financial management focuses on:

- Planned versus actual team / delivery cost.
- Cost per sprint.
- Forecast cost to complete.
- Capacity consumption.
- Investment remaining.
- Cost of delay where relevant.
- Value delivered relative to investment.
- Forecast impact of major scope / dependency decisions.

The objective is to preserve financial discipline without forcing Agile delivery into a fixed-scope predictive control model.

---

## 7. Delivery Forecasting Assumption

Detailed scope is not promised twelve sprints in advance.

Forecasting will instead use empirical evidence such as:

- Historical velocity.
- Available capacity.
- Sprint goal achievement.
- Carry-over.
- Backlog burn-up.
- Throughput.
- Defect trend.
- Dependency status.
- Release readiness.
- Product-outcome metrics.

Forecasts will become more reliable as the team accumulates delivery evidence.

## 8. Product Outcome Assumption

Nova is not considered successful merely because stories are completed.

The initiative will later define measurable outcomes around customer self-service and digital experience.

Potential outcome categories include:

- Reduction in avoidable customer-service contacts.
- Increased self-service completion.
- Reduced abandonment.
- Improved order-status transparency.
- Faster returns / refund visibility.
- Improved notification engagement.
- Improved customer satisfaction for selected journeys.

Exact KPI baselines and targets will be established in a later commit rather than invented in this assumptions document.

---

## 9. Release Assumption

A Sprint Review is not automatically a production release.

A potentially releasable Increment may still be subject to:

- Security / privacy controls.
- Regression testing.
- Enterprise dependency readiness.
- Change / release process.
- Production readiness.
- Operational support readiness.
- Feature flags or phased rollout.

Nova therefore distinguishes:

**Sprint completion → Increment readiness → Release decision → Production rollout**

---

## 10. Agile Governance Principle

Project Nova follows a product-oriented governance model.

The Product Owner owns backlog ordering and value prioritization within agreed boundaries.

The Scrum Team owns the Sprint Backlog and delivery approach.

The Scrum Master / Agile Delivery Lead facilitates flow, removes impediments, supports empirical improvement, and provides transparent delivery reporting.

Senior stakeholders govern:

- Investment.
- Product outcomes.
- Material risks.
- Major dependencies.
- Release commitments.
- Decisions outside normal team authority.
