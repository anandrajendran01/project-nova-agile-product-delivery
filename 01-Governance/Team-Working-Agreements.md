# Project Nova — Team Working Agreements

**Team:** 10-Person Cross-Functional Scrum Team  
**Cadence:** Two-Week Sprints  
**Purpose:** Establish practical team norms that support focus, quality, transparency, and continuous improvement.

---

## 1. Sprint Goal First

The Sprint Goal is the primary shared commitment.

The team may adapt the Sprint Backlog during the Sprint when needed, provided the Sprint Goal remains protected.

If the Sprint Goal becomes invalid or impossible because of a material external event, the Product Owner and Scrum Team will address the situation transparently rather than quietly accumulating carry-over.

---

## 2. No Direct Work Injection

New stakeholder requests enter through the Product Owner and Product Backlog.

Developers do not accept unplanned feature requests directly from stakeholders.

Exception:

A genuine production incident, security event, or mandatory urgent obligation may require immediate action. The impact on Sprint capacity and Sprint Goal must be made visible.

---

## 3. Ready Enough, Not Perfect

Backlog items selected for a Sprint should normally have:

- Clear purpose.
- Testable acceptance criteria.
- known major dependencies.
- sufficient UX / technical context.
- size appropriate for Sprint completion.

The team does not require all uncertainty to disappear before Sprint selection.

---

## 4. Slice Work Small

The team aims to create backlog items that can reach Done within the Sprint.

Large stories should be split by usable customer / business behavior rather than by functional silo where practical.

Recurring unfinished work is treated as a signal to improve slicing, refinement, dependency management, or capacity planning.

---

## 5. Definition of Done Is Non-Negotiable

Work is not counted Done merely because development is complete.

Relevant testing, review, integration, security/privacy checks, telemetry, and documentation must meet the agreed Definition of Done.

Unfinished work returns to the Product Backlog for re-evaluation.

---

## 6. Capacity Is Explicit

Sprint Planning considers:

- Leave.
- holidays.
- operational support load.
- known dependency work.
- planned engineering / quality activities.
- team availability.

The team does not inflate commitments to satisfy a target velocity.

---

## 7. Quality Work Has Capacity

Necessary technical work such as:

- automated testing.
- observability.
- performance improvement.
- security remediation.
- technical debt reduction.
- environment improvement.

is visible in the backlog / Sprint plan when it materially consumes capacity.

Quality is not assumed to happen “for free.”

---

## 8. Dependency Ownership Is Visible

Each material external dependency has:

- an owner.
- expected date / need-by date.
- status.
- impact.
- next action.

Dependencies are raised early rather than only when they block a Sprint.

---

## 9. Decisions Are Recorded When They Matter

Not every team discussion requires formal documentation.

A decision is recorded when it materially affects:

- product direction.
- release scope.
- enterprise dependency.
- architecture.
- investment.
- major risk.
- quality / control obligation.

---

## 10. Metrics Are for Learning

Delivery metrics are used to understand the delivery system.

The team will not:

- compare individual story points.
- set personal velocity targets.
- use velocity as a performance appraisal measure.

Trends such as carry-over, cycle time, throughput, defects, Sprint Goal achievement, and forecast confidence are reviewed in context.

---

## 11. Retrospective Actions Are Small and Owned

Each Retrospective should produce only a manageable number of improvement actions.

Actions should have:

- clear owner.
- intended improvement.
- target Sprint / review point.

Repeatedly carrying the same retrospective action without progress is itself treated as an improvement problem.

---

## 12. Stakeholder Feedback Is Welcome, Priority Ownership Is Clear

Stakeholders are encouraged to challenge assumptions and provide evidence.

The Product Owner remains accountable for Product Backlog ordering.

Feedback does not become committed scope simply because it came from a senior stakeholder.

---

## 13. Release Is Separate from Sprint Completion

A Done Increment may wait for:

- enterprise release window.
- dependency readiness.
- operational readiness.
- security / privacy approval.
- phased rollout decision.

The team preserves a distinction between:

**Done → Releasable → Approved for Production → Released**

---

## 14. Communication Norms

- Raise blockers early.
- Challenge ideas, not individuals.
- Prefer evidence over hierarchy.
- Surface bad news before it becomes a crisis.
- Make assumptions explicit.
- Close decisions with owner and next action.
- Avoid hidden work.
- Avoid creating meetings when an existing Scrum event or asynchronous update is sufficient.

---

## 15. Improvement Review

Working agreements are reviewed during Retrospectives when delivery evidence suggests a norm is no longer useful or a new agreement is required.

They are team agreements, not static policy.
