# Project Nova — Capacity, Cost & Agile Forecasting Framework

**Initiative:** Agile Digital Customer Experience Enhancement  
**Delivery Model:** Scrum / Agile  
**Delivery Horizon:** 12 Two-Week Sprints / 6 Months  
**Core Team:** 10 Members  
**Investment Guardrail:** ₹125.00 L  
**Initial Planned Consumption:** ₹117.00 L  
**Investment Headroom:** ₹8.00 L  
**Status:** Synthetic Portfolio Case Study

> All costs, capacity assumptions, estimates, and forecasts in Project Nova are synthetic and are used solely to demonstrate Agile delivery and financial-governance practices.

---

## 1. Purpose

Project Nova requires financial discipline and forecast transparency without using Project Atlas-style Earned Value Management as the primary delivery-control mechanism.

This framework connects:

- Team availability.
- Sprint capacity.
- Product Backlog refinement.
- Initial story-point forecasting.
- Cost per Sprint.
- Investment consumption.
- Release confidence.
- Forecast-to-complete decisions.

The objective is to answer:

**Can the team deliver the highest-value outcomes within the six-month horizon and ₹125.00 L investment guardrail, and how confident are we in that forecast?**

---

## 2. Investment Model

The approved synthetic investment guardrail is:

**₹125.00 L**

The initial planned operating consumption is:

**₹117.00 L**

This leaves:

**₹8.00 L investment headroom**

The ₹8.00 L is not presented as a predictive-project Management Reserve or BAC contingency.

It is simply uncommitted headroom within the approved Agile investment envelope that may absorb reasonable delivery-cost variation, targeted specialist support, dependency response, or other value-aligned needs.

Any use of that headroom must remain visible in the investment forecast.

## 4. Sprint Cost Logic

Core team and platform costs are relatively stable.

Specialist and release-support costs vary by Sprint.

Planned Sprint cost is therefore:

**Core Team Cost + Platform / Tooling + Specialist Support + Release / Stabilization Support**

This is a financial-consumption forecast, not an Agile productivity score.

Nova deliberately does **not** calculate cost per story point because:

- Story points are relative, team-specific estimates.
- Point scales may change as the team learns.
- Points are not units of customer value.
- Cost-per-point targets can encourage estimate inflation.
- Fixed-team cost does not vary linearly with story-point completion.

---

## 5. Capacity Model

Each Sprint contains approximately ten working days.

With a 10-person team, nominal capacity is:

**100 team-days per Sprint**

Nominal capacity is reduced for known factors such as:

- Leave / holidays.
- Product discovery and research.
- Enterprise coordination.
- Operational support.
- Release / readiness work.
- Training / internal commitments.

Capacity is therefore planned explicitly rather than assuming every Sprint has identical availability.

Story points are **not mechanically converted from team-days**.

Team-days help understand availability; story points help the team forecast relative delivery based on its own sizing system.

## 6. Initial Story-Point Forecast

Nova has no actual team velocity yet.

Therefore, before Sprint execution, the team uses an explicit **initial planning assumption**, not an empirical velocity claim:

**14–18 story points per Sprint for the first four Sprints**

This range is used only as a planning band based on the initial backlog decomposition and synthetic team-capacity assumption.

After Sprint 1 and Sprint 2, the forecast will increasingly use actual Nova delivery evidence.

The first NOW backlog contains:

- **52 story points** of candidate Release 1 work.
- **8 story points** of Release 2 discovery / uncertainty reduction.
- **60 story points total** across the NOW horizon.

Across four Sprints:

- Low planning band: 56 points.
- Base planning case: 60 points.
- Upper planning band: 72 points.

This supports a high initial Release 1 confidence because the 52-point candidate R1 scope fits within the lower four-Sprint planning band, while the additional 8 discovery points remain forecast rather than guaranteed.

---

## 7. Forecasting After Execution Starts

Once actual Sprint evidence exists, Nova will update forecasts using:

- Completed story points / throughput.
- Sprint Goal achievement.
- Carry-over / unfinished work.
- Actual team availability.
- Defect / quality load.
- Dependency status.
- Backlog growth / removal.
- Actual cost to date.
- Remaining planned Sprint cost.
- Product-outcome evidence.

The team should not lock itself to the original 14–18 point assumption if actual evidence indicates a different sustainable delivery range.

---

## 8. Financial Forecast-to-Complete

Nova's financial forecast is based on:

**Actual Cost to Date + Forecast Remaining Cost**

Forecast Remaining Cost considers:

- Stable core-team run rate.
- Known platform costs.
- approved / expected specialist support.
- release-support demand.
- known changes to delivery horizon or capacity.
- material dependency-response costs.

The financial forecast is not derived from story-point completion.

### Initial Position

Before Sprint execution:

- Actual Cost to Date: ₹0.
- Planned Cost to Complete: ₹117.00 L.
- Initial Forecast Total Cost: ₹117.00 L.
- Investment Headroom: ₹8.00 L.
- Forecast Headroom %: 6.4%.

## 9. Financial Escalation Logic

### Normal Management

If forecast cost increases but remains within the ₹125.00 L guardrail:

- identify the driver.
- assess product / delivery impact.
- use scope, specialist, sequencing, or release trade-offs.
- update the forecast transparently.

### Headroom Watch

If forecast total cost reaches approximately **₹121.00 L** or above, the Product & Investment Review should explicitly assess whether remaining headroom is sufficient for the rest of the delivery horizon.

This is an early-warning threshold, not a new authorization limit.

### Formal Escalation

If the forecast is expected to exceed **₹125.00 L**, normal Product Owner backlog trade-offs are no longer sufficient on their own.

The initiative requires a formal investment decision such as:

- Reduce / defer scope.
- Change delivery horizon.
- Approve additional funding.
- Change operating approach.
- Stop lower-value work.

## 11. Capacity and Scope Trade-Offs

When capacity is lower than expected, Nova's default response is not automatically to extend the Sprint or add people.

The Product Owner and team first consider:

- Protect the Sprint Goal.
- Remove / defer lower-value work.
- Split oversized items.
- Change sequence.
- Reduce optional release content.
- Resolve / avoid dependencies.
- Allocate capacity to quality if required.
- update the release forecast.

Adding people late may increase coordination cost and is not assumed to increase capacity immediately.

---

## 12. What Nova Will Not Do

Nova will not:

- Treat story points as hours.
- Convert story points directly into rupees.
- Measure individual productivity using points.
- force constant velocity.
- extend Sprints to complete unfinished work.
- hide lower capacity by inflating estimates.
- treat planned cost as guaranteed actual spend.
- call the ₹117.00 L plan a predictive BAC.
- call the ₹8.00 L headroom Management Reserve.

---

## 13. Management Questions This Framework Supports

At every Product & Investment Review, stakeholders should be able to answer:

1. How much investment has been consumed?
2. What is the current forecast total cost?
3. How much headroom remains?
4. What capacity is available in upcoming Sprints?
5. What actual delivery range is emerging?
6. Which release outcome is most at risk?
7. Which dependencies are affecting confidence?
8. What scope / sequencing trade-off would best protect value?
9. Are quality or product outcomes being sacrificed to hit a forecast?
10. Does any decision exceed the team's investment or governance authority?
